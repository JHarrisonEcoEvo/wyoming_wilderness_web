> "The Cirque of the Towers is one of the great gifts of the mountain gods"
>
> — Steve Bechtel

A fellow by the name of Orrin Bonney gave the Cirque its English name in 1941. He and a few others climbed Pingora peak, the anchor to the scenery of this magnificent valley. Since those days this area has attracted every generation of alpine climbers.

The rock is solid granite, something around 2–3 billion years old from what I can find. It is a joy to touch and climb upon. Since the rock was exposed glaciers have had their way with the terrain, crafting an immensity.

The Wilderness was designated in 1984. Like Cloud Peak, and other mountainous areas of Wyoming, much of the area was protected as a primitive area since the 1930s. During the initial Wilderness designation, Wyoming voiced its dissaproval by passing a joint congressional memorial requesting no more Wilderness. The Senate Majority Leader at the time Don Jewitt (R) is quoted as saying, ["I think we now have wilderness areas up to our necks."](https://www.hcn.org/issues/5.12/). I found this statement in a 1973 issue of High Country News from Lander, Wy.

![Popo Agie To Be Considered](/images/wilderness/popo_agie/hcn-popo-agie-to-be-considered.png)
*High Country News, June 8, 1973*

Thankfully, this landscape was indeed protected as part of the Wyoming Wilderness Act bill of 1984, as championed by [Sen. Malcom Wallop, with Alan Simpson and Dick Cheney co-sponsoring](https://www.congress.gov/bill/98th-congress/senate-bill/543).
